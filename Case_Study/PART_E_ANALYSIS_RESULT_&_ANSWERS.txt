E1. HOW THE SAMPLE WAS ACTUALLY COLLECTED
==========================================

1. Name this sampling method:
   ANSWER: Voluntary Response Sampling (Convenience Sampling)
   
   Why: "We sent the form out on the internal portal and let it open for a week.
   Anyone who wanted to answer, answered."
   
   This is NOT: simple random, stratified random, cluster, or multistage sampling.


2. Did every ITI student have an equal chance of being selected?
   ANSWER: NO
   
   Why:
   - Only students who actively checked the internal portal could see it
   - Only motivated students answered (self-selection bias)
   - Survey was open only 1 week → students who rarely check portal less likely to see it
   - Cairo was pushed in group chat → Cairo students had higher visibility
   
   This violates the definition: "every member has an equal chance of selection"


3. Which students are systematically less likely to have responded?
   ANSWER: Students in smaller branches and students with weaker portal access or lower engagement are less likely to respond.
   
   Branch Response Distribution from the Python data output:
   - Cairo:      100 students (38.5%) — Expected: 52 (20%) — Overrepresented by +48
   - Alexandria:  54 students (20.8%) — Expected: 52 (20%) — Slightly above expectation
   - Menoufia:    32 students (12.3%) — Expected: 52 (20%) — Underrepresented by -20
   - Mansoura:    43 students (16.5%) — Expected: 52 (20%) — Underrepresented by -9
   - Assiut:      31 students (11.9%) — Expected: 52 (20%) — Underrepresented by -21
   
   This means the sample is clearly tilted toward Cairo and away from the smaller branches. The raw data also show that response rates are not spread evenly across branches, which is exactly what creates sampling bias.


4. The memo contains an assumption that could be badly wrong:
   ASSUMPTION: "A few students never filled it in. We assumed they were the
   ones who did not care."
   
   WHY THIS IS WRONG: Non-response ≠ "doesn't care"

   - Never seen the portal notification

   - Had technical problems
   - Did not check internal systems regularly

   HOW THIS BENDS RESULTS:
   The sample is biased toward:
   - Engaged, motivated students from Cairo
   - Students with easier portal access
   - Students more connected to current campus communication channels
   
   IMPACT: It can overestimate the commute problem if the more visible group is also the one with longer travel times.
   - With Cairo in the sample: Mean = 45.30 minutes
   - Without Cairo: Mean = 35.84 minutes
   - Difference: 9.46 minutes
   
   Cairo's average (60.35 min) is the highest branch and pulls the mean up significantly.


E2. DESIGN IT PROPERLY
======================

Population: 2,000 students across 5 branches × 5 tracks

OPTION A: STRATIFIED RANDOM SAMPLING (BETTER ✓)
Definition: Divide into groups (strata), then randomly sample within each group.

Design:
- Strata: 5 branches (Cairo, Alexandria, Menoufia, Mansoura, Assiut)
- Sample size: 260 total
- Within each branch, select students randomly so every student in that branch has a known chance to be included

Why this is better:
- It makes sure all 5 branches are represented
- It reduces branch imbalance such as the Cairo overrepresentation seen in the actual data
- It gives more precise and fair estimates than using a convenience sample
- It is the standard approach for a multi-branch institution

Implementation:
1. Get a complete student list for each branch
2. Randomly select students within each branch
3. Invite by official email or formal student list, not by optional portal promotion
4. Send reminders to improve response rate

OPTION B: CLUSTER RANDOM SAMPLING
Definition: Randomly select some clusters, then survey everyone in those clusters.

Design:
- Randomly pick a small number of branches
- Survey everyone in those branches
- Ignore other branches

Advantages:
✓ Cheaper logistically
✓ Easier to implement

Disadvantages:
✗ Misses unselected branches
✗ Lower precision
✗ Not every student has a fair chance of selection

COMPARISON:
- Stratified sampling: better because it represents all branches and reduces bias
- Cluster sampling: easier, but it can miss important differences between campuses and is less representative

WHICH IS BETTER AND WHY?
STRATIFIED SAMPLING IS CLEARLY BETTER because:

1. Need all 5 branches represented
   - Commute times differ substantially by branch (Cairo: 60.35 min, Assiut: 25.91 min)
   - Cluster sampling may exclude crucial branch differences

2. Reduces bias from overrepresentation
   - The current sample is clearly tilted toward Cairo

3. Gives each student a fair chance within their branch
   - This is closer to the definition of an unbiased sample

4. Produces more stable estimates
   - More reliable comparisons across branches
